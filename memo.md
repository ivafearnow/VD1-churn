**Re:** Which customers to contact – recommendation

## Recommendation

I recommend running a small randomized pilot using the boosted trees model to identify customers at risk of churning. We should randomly split the high-risk contact list into two groups, offering a retention incentive to one group and not the other. Comparing their churn rates will help us measure whether the offer actually prevents customers from leaving.

## Supporting Evidence

The boosted trees model identified 69.4% churners in its top-20% contact list, compared with 39.9% using the current contract rule. The model's AUC advantage over the contract rule has a 95% confidence interval of 0.096 to 0.131, entirely above zero, providing evidence that the improvement is not due to random sampling alone.

However, profitability depends on how many customers the offer actually saves. Our analysis shows that the campaign needs a save rate of at least 13.5% to break even. At a 15% save rate, the hypothetical profit is $670 per 1,000 contacts, but at a 10% save rate, the campaign would lose $1,620.

## Limitations

Although the model predicts which customers are likely to churn, it cannot tell us whether a retention offer will convince them to stay. The save rate and financial assumptions, including the $66 net value and $6.20 contact cost, have not been validated. Additionally, our analysis uses a public dataset rather than Summit Telecom's actual customer data.

## What Would Change My Recommendation

If the pilot shows a save rate below 13.5%, I would recommend reviewing the offer and its costs before expanding the campaign. If the save rate is comfortably above 13.5% and the campaign generates positive returns, I would recommend expanding it gradually while continuing to monitor performance.
